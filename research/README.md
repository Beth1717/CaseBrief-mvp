# Workflow research companion

The existing six-page fillable questionnaire remains the detailed instrument. This short digital companion was derived from its current October 2026 content, especially questions 3–10 and 15–25. New emphasis: contradictory/exculpatory evidence, document scale, three separate verification claims, device constraints, post-pilot costs, onboarding and procurement stages.

Serve the repository with `npm start`, then open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/research/quick-survey.html`. All questions are optional. The form has no network submission, analytics, automatic storage or dependency on a third-party form provider. It exports an explicit JSON download. The return channel and any respondent-identifying information require a separately agreed process; a downloaded answer file does not submit itself.

No survey has been sent, no participant has been recruited and no response is fabricated. This companion is prepared for review; hosting/distribution are separate actions. Do not place completed responses in the public repository.

## Neutral interview prompts

Use the latest period the respondent can describe. Ask what works well before introducing CaseBrief. Obtain their budget range before mentioning competitor prices, trial lengths or desired features. Probe actual events and observed consequences, including zero occurrences; separate measured from estimated time. Ask for an everyday anonymised workflow, never a real case file. Record respondent role/authority, sample and recruitment limitations before making market claims.

A brief introduction: “I’m developing CaseBrief, a case-intelligence workspace for defense teams, and researching where current tools help or create extra work. Would you be willing to answer a few optional questions about your workflow? This is independent product research using no client or case-identifying information. I’m particularly interested in what already works, what falls through the gaps, and what would make another tool unnecessary.”

This is draft outreach text to review, not a sent message. Obtain permission separately for contact, office identification and attributable quotations. Price and privacy questions are research, not offers, commitments or legal consent.
